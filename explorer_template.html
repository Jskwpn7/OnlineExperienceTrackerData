How to read this tool — and its caveats

Independent analysis of Ofcom’s Online Experiences Tracker open data — not an official Ofcom output. It reproduces Ofcom’s published Wave 9 and Wave 10 figures.

What the data is

  • YouGov online survey for Ofcom, twice a year, ~7,000+ people per wave.
  • Represents UK internet users (weighted) — not the whole population. Everything is self-reported.
  • Scope: scams/fraud, deepfakes, impersonation, misinformation only.

Reading the numbers

  • Percentages are weighted; bases shown are unweighted (people asked).
  • Each % is a share of that view’s base — watch the base breadcrumb.
  • Concern (attitude) and Experienced (encounter) are different things.
  • The trend shows a 95% confidence band, and each latest wave-on-wave change is flagged sig. or n.s. CIs use the Kish effective base (accounts for the weighting design effect); they don’t add clustering/finite-population corrections, so treat them as close but not exact.
  • Copy link (top right) copies a link to the exact view you’re on.

Waves & comparability

  • No Wave 3 (Ofcom withdrew it). 13+ (W1–7) vs 18+ (W8–10) — use the Basis toggle (“18+ comparable” is our recompute).
Discontinuity: waves 1–2 (2021–22) used an earlier format and sit far below waves 4+. Treat the 2→4 step as a method change, not a trend.

Base sizes

  • Bases <50 flagged ⚠. Most follow-ups are asked about one sampled harm per person — Total-level only, smaller than headline numbers. Platform-specific (Q22–29) and genAI views are very thin, kept to track over time. Full detail in README.md.
